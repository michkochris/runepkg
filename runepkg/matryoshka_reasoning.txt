Debian's layout is a mix of old and modern conventions: Older Debian/DSL systems keep things split across /lib and /usr/lib, whereas newer Debian uses merged-usr (/lib and /lib64 pointing into /usr/...). That layout mismatch is why dynamic linker paths break if you assume only one layout exists.

.deb packages are simple archives under the hood: A .deb is just an ar archive containing debian-binary, control.tar.* (metadata, dependencies, maintainer scripts), and data.tar.* (the actual files to extract). You don't need heavy Debian tooling just to unpack, inspect, or install them.

Not every package needs the whole Debian world: Debian has lots of meta-packages and policy assumptions meant for full desktop/server upgrades (apt). For a lean rescue/tooling installer like runepkg, treating .deb files as payload + metadata + actual ELF/script needs is much cleaner than trying to mimic every apt habit.
